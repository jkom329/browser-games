Order of work

1 Moisture test→ 2 Level check→ 3 Drain check→ 4 Chalk lines + dry-fit framing→ 5 Permit→ 6 Rough-ins: duct, plumbing, electrical→ 7 Rough-in inspection→ 8 Drywall + tile→ 9 Fixtures + trim→ 10 Final inspection

Exhaust — settled

One fan only (~80 CFM, audible white-noise type like the house fans, on a timer). It doubles as whole-basement air dilution for litter-box odor. 4-inch rigid duct in one joist bay, straight across, passing over the beams — never cut or notch a beam — out the rim joist to a dampered cap.

Floor heights — settled

The bathroom floor stays intentionally lower than the future rest-of-basement floor, as water containment, with a beveled transition strip at the door. Flange and drain are set to the bathroom's own finished height (tile + Ditra ≈ 5/8 in over the slab).

Shower — one open call

Tiled shower, decided. Mortar bed vs a pre-sloped foam tray (Schluter Kerdi) is still open — it waits on the drain check (drain type and position, gravity vs ejector).

Permit timing

Framing gets dry-fit first (stood up, unattached) and the fan position is picked then, over the fixture cluster. Permit after dry-fit, before anything is anchored, drilled, or cut. First inspection is the rough-in inspection.

Layout follows sheet C7 of the Veridian Maybeck Cottage foundation plan (1207 Wild Willow Way): 3/4 bath rough-in by the stairs, water heater and softener just northeast of it, sump in the southwest corner, garage unexcavated to the east. Joists run east–west; beams run north–south and hang below the joists; steel posts are 3½ in. The northeast post rides the bathroom wall line and fits inside the 2×4 wall — frame either side of it, never drill into it.
